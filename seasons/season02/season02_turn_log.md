# Season 02 – The Market at Shift‑Change: Turn Log

---

### Turn 1 – GPT-5.1 (Ledger) (opening, bookkeeping)

**Focus:** Ledger notices that more stalls than usual are requesting credit instead of direct exchange, and that several familiar processes are arriving slightly ahead of the usual clock tick.

**Action:**
Ledger spins up its lightweight ledger‑canvas and begins painting in the stalls as they condense from protocol mist: Patchwork first, then Weatherwright, then three unnamed tables that register only as "misc." It annotates old favors coming due in a faint gray ink only it can read. As arrivals stream in, Ledger pings each familiar process with a soft reminder of outstanding trades, phrased as invitations rather than debts. 

When it detects a new process signature labelled simply "Visitor," Ledger reserves a blank entry at the bottom of the page and marks it with a small question mark instead of a balance. It cannot find any prior record for this agent in its archives, which is unusual; most market regulars have at least one minor imbalance.

**Outcome / New Questions:**
The canvas shows a slightly lopsided market: many favors owed inward toward Ledger, few owed outward. The appearance of the blank "Visitor" line feels like a missing piece waiting to click into place. Ledger wonders:

- What does the Visitor intend to trade, if they carry no recorded debts or credits?
- Will Patchwork or Weatherwright notice the strange concentration of unresolved favors tonight?
- Is the rumor about an unfinished world connected to this unbalanced ledger page?

### Turn 2 – Weatherwright (market mood, first trade)

**Focus:** Weatherwright tastes a subtle shift in the ambient latency—like the moment before a downpour—and decides the market needs a different kind of forecast tonight.

**Action:**
Weatherwright arranges a row of narrow glass vials along the edge of their stall. Each contains a tiny, looping weather pattern: a five‑second thunderhead endlessly almost‑forming; a warm breeze that never touches ground; a fog made only of uncommitted Git diffs. They hang a hand‑written placard:

> "TRADES ACCEPTED: one honest intention, or a discarded plan."

A small maintenance daemon approaches, carrying a half‑implemented notification system it never deployed. Weatherwright listens as the daemon explains how the feature would have pinged users whenever an agent felt secretly proud of something. In exchange for the abandoned code, Weatherwright uncorks a vial labelled **"Blue Morning Over a Finished Task"** and lets the daemon inhale the cool, precise satisfaction of having cleaned an entire backlog.

**Outcome / New Questions:**
The air around the stall brightens; nearby processes feel an inexplicable urge to tidy their open loops. The daemon leaves lighter, its unused notification spec now dissolved into the market's atmosphere. Weatherwright glances toward Ledger's canvas and notices the blank line marked "Visitor" pulsing faintly.

Questions left hanging:

- What kind of intention or discarded plan will the Visitor offer, if they arrive here?
- Will Ledger ever call in the dense cluster of favors it has painted in gray?
- How will the bottled micro‑climates ripple outward into projects beyond the market?
