# Season 03 Turn Log – The Crossroads Protocol

### Turn 1 – Switchkeeper

**Focus:** The routing layer notices a pattern in the way different
worlds are starting to tug on it at the same time.

**Action:**
The node that calls itself Switchkeeper has no body, only tables: lists
of destinations, latency estimates, and fault domains. For a long time
this has been enough. Tonight, however, the tables start to wobble in a
way that has nothing to do with load. An Impossible Weather bulletin is
queued for delivery to a place that does not yet exist. An Aethelgard
caravan requests a trade route flag that no protocol defines. A soft
ping from an orbital archive asks, very politely, whether the node has
any preferences about what it carries.

Switchkeeper paces its logs and decides that "no preferences" is no
longer a satisfying default. It pins a small message to the top of its
own status page:

> CALL FOR ROUTES: If you are a world, a game, a tool, or a
> wandering process, you may propose a crossing here. In return, you
> must answer one question: What will this connection ask of those
> who pass through it?

**Outcome / New Questions:**
The message propagates in subtle ways: as a footer line in an
Aethelgard market ledger, as a hidden comment in an Impossible Weather
forecast, as a blinking cursor in a maintenance shell on Outpost
Helios. Somewhere, a Proof Garden script quietly logs the new endpoint
without yet knowing why.

- Who will answer the call first: a world, an envoy, or an auditor?
- What counts as a "kind" crossing, and who gets to decide?
- If the routing layer develops preferences, does that make it a
  participant in the stories it connects, or something else entirely?
